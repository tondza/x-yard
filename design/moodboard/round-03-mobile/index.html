The six scenarios

1

The Fugitive's turn

Vulture sees their own icon — no ghost, no belief shading. The viewer-dependent rule, from the hidden side. Foot, taxi, bus and a scooter double-move are all drawn along the streets; metro is visibly disabled because n6 is a hub. The log strip shows only what opponents have publicly seen.

2

Agent deducing

The most important screen. No Fugitive is drawn: a ghost at the last-known node, belief shading whose intensity is likelihood, and a top-3 badge. Never a candidate list. The public log — mode only, destination hidden — is the deduction input.

s2-agent-deduction.html →
3

The Reveal Pulse

The dramatic beat: the pulse runs before the Agent phase, so Falcon can still act — and n6 is now reachable this turn. Belief shading is gone because knowledge is exact. This is the tension the whole design rests on.

s3-reveal-pulse.html →
4

Capture and result

A full-screen surface, not a modal — the MVP has zero modals. Capture at turn 12 of 16, pulses survived 2, capture range 1 edge, and tickets spent per seat. Two actions, nothing else.

s4-capture-result.html →
5

Same screen, style H

Deliberately the same state as scenario 2, rendered painterly at night, so the F-versus-H choice can be made on the real MVP screen rather than on a showcase. No fog layer — the director rejected the original H fog as implying a mechanic that does not exist.

s5-agent-deduction-night-H.html →
6

Desktop enhancement

The additive path, proving mobile-first does not paint us into a corner: the same phone Board grown, with the tray becoming a side rail, the log a persistent panel, and the extra width buying map (22 stations around the unmoved original 9) rather than a different game. No hover-only or drag-only affordance.

The design decision these exist to settle

Scenarios 2 and 5 are the same screen in the two surviving art directions. Everything else — layout, information, interaction — is identical, so the only variable is the look.

  • F (scenario 2) is flat vector, 2.5D and daylight. More explicit: paths are labelled, the kit shows which modes have a legal move, and it reads at a glance.
  • H (scenario 5) is painterly, top-down and night. Far more atmospheric; the trade is that belief badges sit further from their stations and the city detail competes with the markers.
  • The product recommendation on file is to ship F and treat H as a later art pass — but that is an engineering convenience argument, and this is ultimately a taste call for the director.

What is not settled

The validator's verdict on the MVP loop is "worth building only if three things change" — as specified the game reduces to pulse-adjacency. The three P0 changes are already applied to these mockups, but the deeper question (is the loop interesting enough, or does it need more Agents, a bigger map, or a second lever between pulses?) is a design call and is recorded in docs/mvp/README.md §5. Also still open: the Reveal Pulse interval (5/10/15 versus 4/8/12/16), starting separation of at least 3 edges, seat assignment, and the fact that nothing yet mechanically enforces that drawn paths follow streets.
